# Bayesian Techniques in Macroeconomics

This graduate course develops the tools required to take a dynamic macroeconomic model from economic mechanisms to Bayesian empirical analysis. The emphasis is on understanding the state-space representation, likelihood, priors, posterior simulation and diagnostics—not merely on running Dynare commands.

**Format:** four sessions; official teaching volume 4 × 4 hours. Four Calendar events are confirmed in November 2026, between 5 and 26 November. The currently recorded slots are 08:30–11:45 and therefore conflict with the declared four-hour duration; final end times will be posted after timetable confirmation.

## Programme

1. Modern business-cycle theory and data: cycle measurement, filtering, shocks, propagation and linearization.
2. A production economy: equilibrium, steady state, Dynare solution, moments and impulse responses.
3. From the model to the likelihood: observables, state-space form, Kalman filter, maximum likelihood and identification.
4. Bayesian estimation of DSGE models: priors, posterior mode, Metropolis–Hastings, diagnostics and posterior economic analysis.

## Assessment

Students choose one of four starting models—involuntary unemployment, a two-country/open-economy model, mortgage markets, or corporate credit—and perform a documented Bayesian estimation. The submission includes reproducible code and a report presenting data, priors, diagnostics, posterior results, economic interpretation and limitations.
