\section{Overview}

Let us suppose that there is two economies $i=\{H,F\}$ of relative size $n$
and $1-n$ respectively.

\subsection{Households}

Suppose a welfare maximizing household enjoying current and future consumption
and dislike working with respective curvatures $\sigma_{C}$, $\sigma_{H}$
$>0$.\ The household maximizes the following stream of utilities discounted at
a fixed rate $\beta$:%
\begin{equation}
\max_{\left\{  C_{t},d_{t},b_{t},H_{t}\right\}  }\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}%
\beta^{\tau}E_{t}\left\{  \frac{\left(  C_{jt+\tau}-X_{t+\tau}\right)
^{1-\sigma_{C}}}{1-\sigma_{C}}-\chi_{i}\frac{H_{jt+\tau}^{1+\sigma_{H}}%
}{1+\sigma_{H}}\right\}
\end{equation}
where $C_{i,t}$\ is consumption, $X_{i,t}$\ is the stock of external habits
$X_{i,t}=hC_{i,t-1}$ with $h\in\lbrack0;1)$ the degree of persistence habits,
$H_{i,t}$ is hours worked and $\chi_{i}>0$\ is a shift parameter allowing to
pin down the amount of hours worked in steady state.

The budget constraint in real terms reads as:%
\begin{equation}
w_{t}H_{jt}+\frac{\Pi_{jt}}{P_{t}^{C}}+\frac{r_{t-1}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}%
d_{jt-1}+e_{t}\frac{r_{t-1}^{\ast}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}b_{jt-1}=C_{jt}+b_{jt}%
+e_{t}b_{jt}^{\ast}+e_{t}\frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}}g\left(  b_{jt}\right)  ,
\end{equation}
where $d_{jt}$\ denotes the acquisition of domestic riskless bonds and
$b_{jt}$\ foreign ones adjusted by the exchange rate $e_{t}$. Households pay
an cost on foreign bonds, this is deemed necessary to remove an unit root
component in consumption dynamics in an open economy environment. Here, the
cost function reads as $g\left(  b_{jt}\right)  =0.5\chi_{B}\left(
b_{jt}-\bar{b}_{j}\right)  ^{2}$.

The Lagrangian problem reads as:%
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{H} &  =\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\beta^{\tau}\left[
\frac{\left(  C_{jt+\tau}-X_{t+\tau}\right)  ^{1-\sigma_{C}}}{1-\sigma_{C}%
}-\chi\frac{H_{jt+\tau}^{1+\sigma_{H}}}{1+\sigma_{H}}\right]  \\
&  +\beta^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{c}\left[  w_{t}H_{jt}+\frac{\Pi_{jt}}%
{P_{t}^{C}}+\frac{r_{t-1}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}d_{jt-1}+e_{t}\frac{r_{t-1}^{\ast}}%
{\pi_{t}^{C}}b_{jt-1}\right]  \\
&  -\beta^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{c}\left[  C_{jt}+d_{jt}+e_{t}b_{jt}%
+\frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}}e_{t}g\left(  b_{jt}\right)  \right]
\end{align*}
where $\lambda_{t}^{c}$ is a lagrangian multiplier.

First order conditions are:%
\begin{align}
C_{jt} &  :\left(  C_{jt}-X_{t}\right)  ^{-\sigma_{C}}-\lambda_{t}^{c}=0\\
d_{jt} &  :\beta E_{t}\left\{  \lambda_{t+1}\right\}  r_{t}/E_{t}\left\{
\pi_{t+1}^{C}\right\}  -\lambda_{t}^{c}=0\\
b_{jt} &  :\beta E_{t}\left\{  \lambda_{t+1}e_{t+1}\right\}  r_{t}^{\ast
}/E_{t}\left\{  \pi_{t+1}^{C}\right\}  -e_{t}\lambda_{t}^{c}=0\\
H_{jt} &  :-\chi H_{jt}^{\sigma_{H}}+\lambda_{t}^{c}w_{t}=0
\end{align}
We define the real exchange rate as:%
\begin{equation}
rer_{t}=e_{t}\frac{P_{t}^{C\ast}}{P_{t}^{C}}.
\end{equation}


The households allocates their consumption on domestically produced goods and
also imports foreign goods.\ The consumption basket of the household:%
\begin{equation}
C_{jt}=\left(  \left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  ^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\left(
c_{jt}\right)  ^{\frac{\mu-1}{\mu}}+\left(  \varepsilon_{t}^{X}\alpha
_{C}\right)  ^{\frac{1}{\mu}}\left(  c_{jt}^{\ast}\right)  ^{\frac{\mu-1}{\mu
}}\right)  ^{\frac{\mu}{\mu-1}}%
\end{equation}
where $\alpha_{C}\in\lbrack0,0.5]$\ is the fraction of foreign goods in the
consumption basket, conversely 1-$\alpha_{C}$ is referred to as the home bias.
In addition, $\varepsilon_{t}^{X}$\ is an AR(1) shock process of the demand
for foreign goods and can be interpreted as an import shock. Parameter
$\mu\geq0$\ is the substitution degree between home goods and foreign goods,
when $\mu$\ is high, households are more sensible to price differential
between home and foreign goods, and will reduce their imports.

The price index of goods reads as:%
\begin{equation}
P_{t}^{C}=\left[  \left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  P_{t}\right)  ^{1-\mu
}+\alpha_{C}\left(  e_{t}P_{t}^{\ast}\right)  ^{1-\mu}\right]  ^{\frac
{1}{1-\mu}}%
\end{equation}
Optimal demands for home $c_{jt}$ and foreign $c_{jt}^{\ast}$ varieties are
given by:%
\begin{equation}
c_{jt}=\left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  \frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}}\right)
^{-\mu}C_{jt}\text{ and }c_{jt}^{\ast}=\varepsilon_{t}^{X}\alpha_{C}\left(
\frac{e_{t}P_{t}^{\ast}}{P_{t}^{C}}\right)  ^{-\mu}C_{jt}%
\end{equation}
Here, $c_{jt}^{\ast}$\ are imports.

\subsection{Retailers}

Final firms competitively pack different types of good varieties $i$\ drawn on
an unitary continuum to produce an homogeneous good sold to households. This
firm is necessary when introducing monopolistic competition as they allows to
create an elastic demand curve that intermediate firms exploits to generate
positive profits.\ Production of final firms employs the following CES packing
technology:%
\begin{equation}
Y_{t}=\left(
%TCIMACRO{\dint \nolimits_{0}^{1}}%
%BeginExpansion
{\displaystyle\int\nolimits_{0}^{1}}
%EndExpansion
y_{it}^{\left(  \epsilon-1\right)  /\epsilon}\text{d}i\right)  ^{\epsilon
/\left(  \epsilon-1\right)  } \label{eq final firms}%
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon>1$\ is the perfect substitutability between different types of
varieties $i$. The economy converges to a competitive equilibrium for
$\epsilon\rightarrow1$, while for any $\epsilon>1$, goods are imperfect
substitutes which allows firms to rise their selling price above their
marginal cost.

Final \ firms are perfectly competitive and maximize profits, $P_{t}%
Y_{t}-p_{it}y_{it},$, under their technology constraint in
\autoref{eq final firms} to obtain the optimal demand constraint for the i-th
good:%
\begin{equation}
y_{it}=\left(  \frac{p_{it}}{P_{t}}\right)  ^{-\epsilon}Y_{t}.
\end{equation}
While the zero profit condition reads as: $P_{t}=(%
%TCIMACRO{\tint \nolimits_{0}^{1}}%
%BeginExpansion
{\textstyle\int\nolimits_{0}^{1}}
%EndExpansion
p_{it}^{1-\epsilon}$d$i)^{1/(1-\epsilon)}.$

\subsection{Intermediate firms}

Let us now suppose that there is a large number of identical firms. They hire
workers and combine them with capital (both produced by them and supplied by
households). Intermediate firms' technology is given by:%
\begin{equation}
Y_{it}=\varepsilon_{t}^{Z}H_{it}^{\alpha}%
\end{equation}


Profits of the intermediate firm i are given by:%
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{it}=P_{it}\varepsilon_{t}^{Z}H_{it}^{\alpha}-W_{t}H_{it}%
\end{equation}
Intermediate firms solve a two stage problem.\ First they minimize input costs
on a perfectly competitive market.\ First order condition provides the
marginal cost:%
\begin{equation}
MC_{it}=\frac{H_{it}}{\alpha Y_{it}}W_{t}%
\end{equation}


Second, firms face price adjustment costs paid in terms of home goods $P_{t}%
$.\ These are quadractic adjustment costs:%
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{it}^{P}=\frac{\chi_{P}}{2}\left(  \frac{P_{it+\tau}}{P_{it-1+\tau}%
}-1\right)  ^{2}%
\end{equation}


The profit optimization becomes dynamic, intermediates set prices facing
nominal rigidities by maximizing the discount sum of real profits:%

\begin{equation}
\max_{p_{it}}%
%TCIMACRO{\dsum \limits_{\tau=0}^{\infty}}%
%BeginExpansion
{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{\infty}}
%EndExpansion
\beta^{\tau}\frac{\lambda_{t+\tau}^{c}}{\lambda_{t}^{c}}\left[  \frac
{P_{it+\tau}}{P_{t+\tau}^{C}}\left(  \frac{P_{it+\tau}}{P_{t+\tau}}\right)
^{-\epsilon}-\varepsilon_{t+\tau}^{P}mc_{t+\tau}\left(  \frac{P_{it+\tau}%
}{P_{t+\tau}}\right)  ^{-\epsilon}-\frac{P_{t+\tau}}{P_{t+\tau}^{C}}\frac
{\chi_{P}}{2}\left(  \frac{P_{it+\tau}}{P_{it-1+\tau}}-1\right)  ^{2}\right]
Y_{t+\tau}%
\end{equation}
where $mc_{t}$ is the marginal cost and $\varepsilon_{t}^{P}$\ is an AR(1)
price shock.

First order condition, assuming symmetry (i.e.\ $P_{t}=P_{it}$) :%
\begin{equation}
\left(  1-\epsilon\right)  +\epsilon\varepsilon_{t}^{P}mc_{t}-\chi_{P}\pi
_{t}\left(  \pi_{t}-1\right)  +\beta\frac{\lambda_{t+1}^{c}}{\lambda_{t}^{c}%
}\frac{Y_{t+1}}{Y_{t}}\chi_{P}\pi_{t+1}\left(  \pi_{t+1}-1\right)  =0
\end{equation}


\subsection{Authorities}

We use a \cite{taylor1993discretion} rule-type to model the setting of the
nominal interest rate by the monetary authority:%
\begin{equation}
R_{t}=R_{t-1}^{\rho}\left(  \bar{R}\left(  \frac{\pi_{t}^{C}}{\bar{\pi}^{C}%
}\right)  ^{\phi_{\pi}}\left(  \frac{Y_{t}}{\bar{Y}}\right)  ^{\phi_{y}%
}\right)  ^{1-\rho}\varepsilon_{t}^{R}%
\end{equation}
where $\rho\in\lbrack0,1)$ is the smoothing coefficient of the monetary policy
rule, $\phi_{\pi}$\ the reaction to the inflation gap and $\phi_{y}$\ the
reaction to the ouput gap. Steady states for interest rates, inflation and
output are respectively $\bar{R}$, $\bar{\pi}$ and $\bar{Y}$. Finally,
$\varepsilon_{t}^{R}$\ is a monetary policy shock.

\subsection{Equilibrium conditions}

First, the nominal exchange rate and real exchange rates in both sides of each
countries are related by:%
\[
e_{t}=1/e_{t}^{\ast}\text{ \ and \ }rer_{t}=1/rer_{t}^{\ast}%
\]
we can thus substitute for the foreign country and express our model in terms
of $e_{t}$ and $rer_{t}$.

Resource constraints is given by the total demand for home goods from home and
foreign households (including also adjustment costs)%
\begin{align}
\int_{0}^{n}Y_{it}\text{d}i &  =\int_{0}^{n}c_{jt}\text{d}j+\int_{n}^{1}%
c_{jt}^{\ast}\text{d}j+\int_{0}^{n}\Delta_{t}^{P}Y_{jt}\text{d}j+\int_{0}%
^{n}e_{t}g\left(  b_{t}\right)  \text{d}j\\
nY_{t} &  =n\left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  \frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}%
}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}+\left(  1-n\right)  \varepsilon_{t}^{X\ast}%
\alpha_{C}\left(  \frac{P_{t}}{e_{t}P_{t}^{C\ast}}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}%
C_{t}^{\ast}+n\Delta_{t}^{P}Y_{t}+ng\left(  b_{t}\right)  \\
Y_{t} &  =\left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  \frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}}\right)
^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}+\frac{\left(  1-n\right)  }{n}\varepsilon_{t}^{X\ast}%
\alpha_{C}\left(  \frac{P_{t}}{e_{t}P_{t}^{C\ast}}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}%
C_{t}^{\ast}+\Delta_{t}^{P}Y_{t}+\frac{\chi_{B}}{2}\left(  b_{t}-\bar
{b}\right)  ^{2}%
\end{align}
Similarly for the foreign country:%
\[
\int_{n}^{1}Y_{it}^{\ast}\text{d}i=\int_{n}^{1}c_{jt}^{\ast}\text{d}j+\int
_{1}^{n}c_{jt}\text{d}j+\int_{n}^{1}\Delta_{t}^{P}Y_{jt}\text{d}j+\int_{n}%
^{1}\frac{\chi_{B}}{2}\left(  b_{jt}^{\ast}-\bar{b}_{j}\right)  ^{2}\text{d}j
\]
To get the accumulation of net foreign asset, we aggregate the household
constraint and assume the amount of domestic bonds is zero, $B_{t}=B_{t-1}%
=0$:
\[
e_{t}nB_{t}-e_{t}R_{t-1}^{\ast}nB_{t-1}=W_{t}nH_{t}+n\Pi_{t}-P_{t}^{C}%
nC_{t}-P_{t}ng\left(  b_{t}\right)
\]
Recall that with a zero profit condition over inputs markets, we have,
$MC_{t}Y_{t}=W_{t}H_{t}$.\ While on final goods, a markup is applied net of
ajustment costs of prices, $\Pi_{t}=\left[  P_{t}-MC_{t}-\Delta_{t}%
^{P}\right]  Y_{t}$.\ We find after rewriting and injecting $Y_{t}$:%
\begin{align*}
e_{t}B_{t}-e_{t}R_{t-1}^{\ast}B_{t-1} &  =MC_{t}Y_{t}+\left[  P_{t}%
-MC_{t}-P_{t}\Delta_{t}^{P}\right]  Y_{t}-P_{t}^{C}C_{t}-P_{t}g\left(
b_{t}\right)  \\
&  =P_{t}Y_{t}-P_{t}\Delta_{t}^{P}Y_{t}-P_{t}^{C}C_{t}-P_{t}g\left(
b_{t}\right)  \\
&  =P_{t}\left[  \left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  \frac{P_{t}}{P_{t}^{C}%
}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}+\varepsilon_{t}^{X\ast}\alpha_{C}\left(
\frac{P_{t}}{e_{t}P_{t}^{C\ast}}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}^{\ast}\frac{1-n}%
{n}\right]  -P_{t}^{C}C_{t}%
\end{align*}
This equation determines the quantity of foreign asset at the end of period,
this way the left hand side of the equation needs to be rewritten in order to
take into account the timing effect of the echange rate over the previous
stock of foreign debt.\ In particular, an increase in the foreign echange rate
$e_{t}/e_{t-1}>1$ generates a wealth effect for the domestic household by
boosting his financial revenues.\ The left hand side including this end of
period wealth effect from changes in the nominal exchange rate reads as,
$B_{t}^{\ast}-e_{t}/e_{t-1}R_{t-1}^{\ast}B_{t-1}^{\ast}.$ In real terms, the
final equation of net foreign assets is given by:%
\begin{equation}
b_{jt}=\frac{R_{t-1}^{\ast}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}\Delta e_{t}b_{jt-1}+\frac{P_{t}%
}{P_{t}^{C}}\left(  \left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  \frac{P_{t}}%
{P_{t}^{C}}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}+\varepsilon_{t}^{X\ast}\alpha_{C}\left(
\frac{P_{t}}{e_{t}P_{t}^{\ast}}\right)  ^{-\mu_{C}}C_{t}^{\ast}\frac{1-n}%
{n}\right)  -C_{t}%
\end{equation}
In a world populated by two countries, international financial markets are
closed by:%
\begin{equation}
nb_{t}+\left(  1-n\right)  b_{t}^{\ast}=0
\end{equation}


\section{Determinacy of the equilibrium}

For determinacy purpose, some variables need to be rewritten to be
stationnary.\ In particular, prices and the exchange rate are not stationnary,
thus requiring to express (i) the nominal exchange rate is variations $\Delta
e_{t}$, and (ii) prices in a relative fashion $p_{t}=P_{t}/P_{t}^{C}$.

First, the relative price index can be expressed in growth rates to be
stationnary:%
\begin{equation}
\frac{p_{t}}{p_{t-1}}=\frac{\pi_{t}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}%
\end{equation}


Second, recall that $rer_{t}=e_{t}P_{t}^{C\ast}/P_{t}^{C}$, then we can
express the real exchange in terms of growth too:%
\begin{equation}
\frac{rer_{t}}{rer_{t-1}}=\Delta e_{t}\frac{\pi_{t}^{C\ast}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}%
\end{equation}
And the FOC on foreign bonds can also be written in terms of variations of the
nominal exchange rate:%
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_{t}\left\{  \Delta e_{t+1}\right\}  =\left(  1+\chi_{B}%
rer_{t}\left(  b_{t}-\bar{b}\right)  \right)  \frac{R_{t}}{R_{t}^{\ast}}%
\end{equation}


Third, dividing the CES\ price index by $P_{t}^{C}$, the following relation
emerges:
\begin{equation}
1=\left[  \left(  1-\alpha\right)  p_{t}^{1-\mu}+\alpha rer_{t}^{1-\mu_{C}%
}\right]  ^{\frac{1}{1-\mu}}%
\end{equation}
In addition, relative price setting affect the following equations:%
\begin{align}
Y_{t} &  =\left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  p_{t}^{-\mu}C_{t}+\frac{1-n}{n}%
\alpha_{C}\left(  \frac{p_{t}}{rer_{t}}\right)  ^{-\mu}C_{t}^{\ast}%
+0.5\chi_{P}\left(  \pi_{t}-1\right)  ^{2}Y_{t}+g\left(  b_{t}\right)  \\
b_{t}^{\ast} &  =\frac{R_{t-1}^{\ast}}{\pi_{t}^{C}}\Delta e_{t}b_{t-1}^{\ast
}+p_{t}\left(  \left(  1-\alpha_{C}\right)  \left(  p_{t}\right)  ^{-\mu}%
C_{t}+\frac{1-n}{n}\varepsilon_{t}^{X\ast}\alpha_{C}\left(  \frac{p_{t}%
}{rer_{t}}\right)  ^{-\mu}C_{t}^{\ast}\right)  -C_{t}%
\end{align}
