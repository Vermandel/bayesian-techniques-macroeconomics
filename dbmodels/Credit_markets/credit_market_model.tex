\section{The model}

I\ develop here a toy model of credit market for firms that relies on the
collateral constraint mechanism \textit{\`{a} la} \cite{kiyotaki1997credit}.
See \cite{gerali2010credit} and \cite{iacoviello2015financial} for estimated
version of the collateral constraint model for industrial loans.

\subsection{Households}

Suppose a welfare maximizing household enjoying current and future consumption
and dislike working with respective curvatures $\sigma_{C}$, $\sigma_{H}$
$>0$.\ The household maximizes the following stream of utilities discounted at
a fixed rate $\beta_{H}$:%
\begin{equation}
\max_{\left\{  C_{t}^{H},d_{t},H_{t}\right\}  }\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\beta
_{H}^{\tau}E_{t}\left\{  \frac{\left(  C_{t+\tau}^{H}\right)  ^{1-\sigma_{C}}%
}{1-\sigma_{C}}-\chi\frac{H_{t+\tau}^{1+\sigma_{H}}}{1+\sigma_{H}}\right\}
\end{equation}
where $C_{t}^{H}$\ is consumption, $H_{t}$ is hours worked and $\chi>0$\ is a
shift parameter allowing to pin down the amount of hours worked in steady state.

The budget constraint in real terms reads as:%
\begin{equation}
C_{t}^{H}+d_{t}=R_{t-1}d_{t-1}+H_{t}w_{t}-T_{t},
\end{equation}
where $d_{t}$ is the amount of real deposits, $R_{t-1}$ is the real interest
rate of real deposits $d_{t}$, $w_{t}$ the real wage is computed as
$w_{t}=W_{t}/P_{t}$ and a lump-sum tax financing the government $T_{t}$.

The Lagrangian problem reads as:%
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{H}  &  =\sum_{\tau=0}^{\infty}\beta^{\tau}\left[
\frac{\left(  C_{t+\tau}^{H}\right)  ^{1-\sigma_{C}}}{1-\sigma_{C}}-\chi
\frac{H_{t+\tau}^{1+\sigma_{H}}}{1+\sigma_{H}}\right] \\
&  +\beta^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{H}\left[  \frac{R_{t-1+\tau}}{\pi_{t+\tau}%
}d_{t-1+\tau}+H_{t+\tau}w_{t+\tau}-C_{t+\tau}^{H}-d_{t+\tau}-T_{t+\tau
}\right]
\end{align*}
where $\lambda_{t}^{H}$ is a lagrangian multiplier.

First order conditions are:%
\begin{align}
C_{t}^{H}  &  :\left(  C_{t}^{H}\right)  ^{-\sigma_{C}}-\lambda_{t}^{H}=0\\
d_{t}  &  :\beta_{H}E_{t}\left\{  \lambda_{t+1}^{H}\right\}  R_{t}-\lambda
_{t}^{H}=0\\
H_{t}  &  :-\chi H_{t}^{\sigma_{H}}+\lambda_{t}^{H}w_{t}=0
\end{align}


\subsection{Entrepreneurs}

Let us now suppose that there is a large number of identical entrepreneurs
which are credit constraint. They hire workers and combine them with capital
(both produced by them and supplied by households).\ As in
\cite{iacoviello2015financial}, entrepreneurs are subject to a borrowing
constraint that acts as a wedge on the capital and labor demand:
\begin{equation}
l_{t}=m_{K}E_{t}\left\{  q_{t+1}\right\}  \frac{K_{t}}{r_{t}^{E}}-m_{H}%
h_{t}w_{t}%
\end{equation}
where $l_{t}$\ is the real stock of real loans, entrepreneurs cannot borrow
more than a fraction $m_{K}\geq0$ of the expected value of their real capital
stock, given by $E_{t}\left\{  q_{t+1}\right\}  K_{t}/r_{t}^{E}$. Here,
$q_{t+1}$ is the expected selling price of capital goods, if shocks between t
and t+1 are too large, financial intermediaries must be strongly binding and
generate a large recession. Variable $r_{t}^{E}$\ is the real borrowing rate.
This was one of the key result of \cite{kiyotaki1997credit} (\textit{i.e.}%
\ small shocks, large recessions). In addition, the borrowing constraint
stipulates that a fraction $m_{H}\geq0$ of the wage bill must be paid in advance.

In the same vein as for households, the welfare function discounted at a rate
$\beta_{E}$ (%
%TCIMACRO{\TEXTsymbol{<}}%
%BeginExpansion
$<$%
%EndExpansion
$\beta_{H}$) reads as:
\begin{equation}
\max_{\left\{  C_{t}^{E},K_{t},I_{t},L_{t},h_{t}\right\}  }\sum_{\tau
=0}^{\infty}\beta_{E}^{\tau}E_{t}\left\{  \frac{\left(  C_{t+\tau}^{E}\right)
^{1-\sigma_{C}}}{1-\sigma_{C}}\right\}
\end{equation}
where $C_{t}^{E}$\ is the consumption of entrepreneurs.

Firms technology is given by:%
\begin{equation}
Y_{t}=\varepsilon_{t}^{A}K_{t-1}^{\alpha}h_{t}^{1-\alpha}%
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_{t}^{A}$ is the a technology shock, $K_{t-1}$\ is the
physical stock of capital with fixed intensity of $\alpha\in\lbrack0;1]$ and
$h_{t}$\ is the labor demand.

The balance sheet of the entrepreneur reads as:%
\begin{equation}
C_{t}^{E}+I_{t}+w_{t}h_{t}+r_{t-1}^{E}l_{t-1}=Y_{t}+l_{t}%
\end{equation}
where $I_{t}$ is investment, $w_{t}$\ real wage.

The accumulation oh physical capital is given by:%
\begin{equation}
I_{t}\varepsilon_{t}^{I}\left(  1-S\left(  \frac{I_{t}}{I_{t-1}}\right)
\right)  =K_{t}-\left(  1-\delta\right)  K_{t-1}%
\end{equation}
where $S\left(  \cdot\right)  $ is an adjustment cost function on investment
introduced by \cite{christiano2005nominal}.\ This function $S\left(  x\right)
=0.5\kappa\left(  x-\bar{x}\right)  ^{2}$ features some congestion cost when
entrepreneurs are simulteaneously investing at the same time. Parameter
$\kappa\geq0$\ is the degree of these adjustment costs. Capital stock is
depreciating at a fixed rate of $\delta\in\lbrack0,1]$.

The Lagrangian faced by entrepreneurs reads as:%
\begin{align*}
\mathcal{L}_{t}^{E}  &  =%
%TCIMACRO{\dsum \limits_{\tau=0}^{\infty}}%
%BeginExpansion
{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\tau=0}^{\infty}}
%EndExpansion
\beta_{E}^{\tau}\frac{\left(  C_{t+\tau}^{E}\right)  ^{1-\sigma_{C}}}%
{1-\sigma_{C}}\\
&  +\beta_{E}^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{E}\left[  Y_{t+\tau}+l_{t+\tau
}-C_{t+\tau}^{E}-I_{t+\tau}-w_{t+\tau}h_{t+\tau}-r_{t-1+\tau}^{E}l_{t-1+\tau
}\right] \\
&  +\beta_{E}^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{E}\varphi_{t+\tau}^{E}\left[  m_{K}%
E_{t}\left\{  q_{t+1+\tau}\right\}  \frac{K_{t+\tau}}{r_{t+\tau}^{E}}%
-m_{H}h_{t+\tau}w_{t+\tau}-l_{t+\tau}\right] \\
&  +\beta_{E}^{\tau}\lambda_{t+\tau}^{E}q_{t+\tau}\left[  K_{t+\tau}-\left(
1-\delta\right)  K_{t-1+\tau}-I_{t+\tau}\varepsilon_{t+\tau}^{I}\left(
1-S\left(  I_{t+\tau}/I_{t-1+\tau}\right)  ^{2}\right)  \right]
\end{align*}
where $\lambda_{t}^{E}$, $\varphi_{t}^{E}$ and $q_{t}$\ are lagrangian
multipliers.\ Here, we simplify the problem for two last constraints by
pre-multiplying to express each constraints into consumption equivalents.

First order conditions are given by:%
\begin{align}
C_{t}^{E}  &  :\left(  C_{t}^{E}\right)  ^{-\sigma_{C}}-\lambda_{t}^{E}=0\\
l_{t}  &  :\lambda_{t}^{E}-\beta_{E}E_{t}\left\{  \lambda_{t+1}^{E}\right\}
r_{t}^{E}-\lambda_{t}^{E}\varphi_{t}^{E}=0\\
I_{t}  &  :-\lambda_{t}^{E}-\lambda_{t}^{E}q_{t}\varepsilon_{t}^{I}\left(
1-\frac{\partial S\left(  I_{t}/I_{t-1}\right)  }{\partial I_{t}}\right)
+\beta_{E}\lambda_{t+1}^{E}q_{t+1}\varepsilon_{t+1}^{I}\frac{\partial S\left(
I_{t+1}/I_{t}\right)  }{\partial I_{t}}=0\\
K_{t}  &  :\beta_{E}E_{t}\left\{  \lambda_{t+1}^{E}\left[  q_{t+1}\left(
1-\delta\right)  +\alpha\frac{Y_{t+1}}{K_{t}}\right]  \right\}  -q_{t}%
\lambda_{t}^{E}+\lambda_{t}^{E}\varphi_{t}^{E}E_{t}\left\{  q_{t+1}\right\}
\frac{m_{K}}{r_{t}^{E}}=0\\
h_{t}  &  :\left[  \left(  1-\alpha\right)  \frac{Y_{t}}{h_{t}}-w_{t}\right]
\lambda_{t}^{E}-\lambda_{t}^{E}\varphi_{t}^{E}m_{H}w_{t}=0
\end{align}
Rewriting these conditions dividing by $\lambda_{t}^{E}$\ provides:
\begin{align}
\lambda_{t}^{E}  &  =\left(  C_{t}^{E}\right)  ^{-\sigma_{C}}\\
1-\varphi_{t}^{E}  &  =\beta_{E}E_{t}\left\{  \frac{\lambda_{t+1}^{E}}%
{\lambda_{t}^{E}}\right\}  r_{t}^{E}\\
q_{t}\varepsilon_{t}^{I}  &  =1+q_{t}\varepsilon_{t}^{I}\frac{\kappa}%
{2}\left(  1+\left(  3\frac{I_{t}}{I_{t-1}}-4\right)  \frac{I_{t}}{I_{t-1}%
}\right)  +\beta_{E}\left\{  \frac{\lambda_{t+1}^{E}}{\lambda_{t}^{E}}%
q_{t+1}\varepsilon_{t+1}^{I}\kappa\left(  1-\frac{I_{t}}{I_{t-1}}\right)
\left(  \frac{I_{t}}{I_{t-1}}\right)  ^{2}\right\} \\
r_{t}^{E}q_{t}  &  =\left(  1-\varphi_{t}^{E}\right)  E_{t}\left\{  \left[
q_{t+1}\left(  1-\delta\right)  +\alpha\frac{Y_{t+1}}{K_{t}}\right]  \right\}
+\varphi_{t}^{E}m_{K}E_{t}\left\{  q_{t+1}\right\} \\
w_{t}  &  =\frac{\left(  1-\alpha\right)  }{\left(  1+\varphi_{t}^{E}%
m_{H}\right)  }\frac{Y_{t}}{h_{t}}%
\end{align}
The key friction here is the collateral constraint, whose multiplier
$\varphi_{t}^{E}$, provides the shadow cost of one unit of loans and affect in
turn the overall discounting of households. When the friction binds a lot,
with high value of $\varphi_{t}^{E}$, households are actually getting more
patient and will consume less and invest less, that is mechanically creating
the macroeconomic conditions for recession.

\subsection{Financial intermediaries}

Here, we assume perfectly competitive and frictionless financial
intermediaries, which collect deposits to households and provide loans to
entrepreneurs:%
\begin{equation}
\Pi_{t}^{B}=r_{t}^{E}l_{t}+r_{t}b_{t}-r_{t}d_{t}%
\end{equation}
facing the following balance sheet:%
\[
d_{t}=l_{t}+b_{t}%
\]
The first order condition reads as:%
\[
r_{t}^{E}=r_{t}%
\]
so there is no wedge between the real lending rate and the real deposit rate.

One of the key contributions of \cite{gerali2010credit} and
\cite{iacoviello2015financial} is to introduce imperfect financial intermediaries.

\subsection{Equilibrium conditions}

Aggregate consumption reads as:%
\begin{equation}
C_{t}=C_{t}^{E}+C_{t}^{H}.
\end{equation}


And total demand is given by:%
\begin{equation}
Y_{t}=C_{t}+I_{t}+G_{t}.
\end{equation}


\subsection{The friction in steady state}

The key friction here is the binding constraint. How are we sure that this
friction is actually binding? To be sure that the friction is binding, we need
to check whether in the deterministic steady state $\bar{\varphi}^{E}>0$, if
so then the constraint is eternally binding. First, let's remove time
subscripts to Euler equations:%
\begin{align*}
\beta_{H}\bar{r}-1  &  =0\\
1-\beta_{E}\bar{r}-\bar{\varphi}^{E}  &  =0
\end{align*}
Combining these two equations through their common interest rate:%
\[
\bar{\varphi}^{E}=1-\frac{\beta_{E}}{\beta_{H}}%
\]
The constraint binds as long as $\beta_{I}/\beta_{H}<1$, $\bar{\varphi}^{E}>0$.%
